If I can do it, anybody can

starting a career in AI at 27, from Tunisia

001 · 16 nov 2026 · 7 min read · midnightblue

When I was a kid I wanted to be an astronaut. I hadn't even explored Earth yet and I was already so eager to go beyond it, out into space. My curiosity had no limits.

I read a lot of stories about inventors, the people who made things that didn't exist before. And honestly I recognized myself in them, the same curiosity, the same way of processing things. So in my kid brain the math was simple: if I shape my life the way they shaped theirs, I can do what they did.

I truly believe that when we're kids our imagination of what's achievable is limitless. It really is. Then society and culture slowly bring it down to what's "realistic," to what tools you have, what you know, where you were born.

high school

For me the moment was high school. The social ladder, people doing dumb shit to fit in, and me just confused the whole time.

I wasn't bullied. But I was mocked, for doing good at school, for nerding out about stuff, for this, for that. Achievements get laughed at before you even do them. No encouragement. And when you make it somehow, the people around you don't really support you either.

Creating things used to give me validation and dopamine. Then it just didn't anymore.

Don't get me wrong, I'm not playing the victim here. I'm not one. As humans we can adapt to any circumstances. But sometimes the environment is just disabling, and you end up seeking more.

it wasn't social anxiety

For years I thought I had social anxiety. I didn't. I wasn't anxious because of people. I was anxious because I deeply felt like I didn't belong, like a stranger, an alien.

So I tried to act more like people. And it turned me self-destructive. I'm pretty sure I'm on the spectrum, and I was masking without even being aware of it. Masking is draining, man. I started doing other stuff for dopamine, stuff that didn't give anything back.

When all I really wanted to do was create.

failing

After high school I wasn't the best student. I never asked for help, but I always tried. And I failed, and I failed.

Honestly, I believe failure teaches you so much more than success. I had success, financial success, and I learned nothing from it. I also believe from the bottom of my heart that luck is a huge factor. That doesn't take away hard work. But smart work is better than hard work.

I have a CS degree, finished it at 25. Realistically? I know less computer science than that paper says. Not nothing, but I was going through the motions of education the same way I was going through the motions of life. What the degree actually taught me is resilience. Going forward even when you feel your guts ripping out of your body and you're completely lost.

And if there's one thing I learned from all of it, it's this: actions matter more than anything. Feelings can be fixed and changed through actions. It's not even about control.

It's about taking charge of how you feel about the world, not how the world makes you feel.

trying the doors in front of me

I didn't just wake up and go "I'll do AI." I tried to integrate into society first. Physical jobs, trading, figuring out the entrepreneur world.

I tried to start a car rental agency. You can't. The legal minimum here is 15 cars. I've got two lol. So I rent them through an existing agency, which controls almost everything, and the guy who owns it is pretty obviously trying to make my life harder. It's monopolies here, and the law protects the people who've been in the game the longest.

The system isn't just rigged, it's almost rural. And again, I'm not blaming it. But it's real.

Tunisia has its advantages too, don't get me wrong. The weather, the nature, the pace of life. Life here is slower, nobody's in a rush.

Except me sometimes. I'm 27, and some days I feel late to everything.

open world

I grew up gaming, and I truly believe we live in an open world. The area you spawned in might be small, closed doors and gatekeepers everywhere, but the map is way bigger than that.

The computer has always been my environment. I'm not an introvert by nature, honestly, I grew to become one. Behind a screen is where I feel most like me.

why AI

This is my honest theory right now, and I'll keep updating it as I learn.

I'm bad at executing. I lean more towards managing: seeing the big picture, deciding what should exist, organizing how it gets built. All I ever wanted was to be a creator. Not just creative, I'm creatively practical. I want to build things that are actually useful, my way.

And I feel like AI is what finally lets someone like me do that. It doesn't skip the learning, I still need to understand what gets built and I'm learning it properly. But it lets me direct, build and ship things I could never have built alone a few years ago.

The goal: a remote job at a company building AI agents and tools. A real team, real structure, where the only objective is to build something and scale it. That's the dream. This blog is the road.

where I'm at

I started a few days ago. So far:

  • I built my first real project, a maintenance tracker for my car. It's a 2004 Mercedes W211 and yes I love it way too much. It logs every service and soon it'll tell me what's due.
  • I tried writing my first "spec," basically describing in plain English what a piece of code should do. My honest answer was: "no clue." So yeah, learning that too.
  • I stopped a session early because I wasn't in the right headspace. Turns out knowing when to stop is part of it.

Small stuff. But it's real, and it's mine.

why I'm writing this

This blog is me being as honest as possible with myself. I feel like if you don't express yourself, yourself ceases to exist. So I'm kind of fighting for existence here, in a world where attention is a currency. I never liked a lot of attention, but as a human being I want to feel like I'm here.

Honestly, just answering the questions that turned into this post, I felt heard. I felt like I exist. That's already worth it.

And maybe someone reading this, in a worse position, a better one, whatever, it's all subjective, gets something out of it. I don't need you to feel anything. I just want you to read this and know:

I have a CS degree and I'm basically starting from scratch. I'm willing to learn no matter what everybody else thinks or has told me. And if I can do it, anybody can.

That's literally how I feel.

See you in the next one.
